WELCOME TO THE TABLE

Make the game your own.

  1. Choose New game. Set team count, names, the starting team, answer format, and question style.
  2. Select difficulty, Bible scope, and 1–12 categories. Short boards have three clues per category; full boards have five. Play-to-target keeps scores across fresh boards until a team reaches the target.
  3. Choose no timer or 15, 30, or 60 seconds. Opening a clue starts its timer; closing the dialog does not reset it. Pause while judging a spoken answer. A timeout counts as incorrect.
  4. Choose full, half, or zero wrong-answer penalties. Rotating turns end the clue after any result. Winner-keeps-control retains a correct team's turn. Steals give every team at most one attempt; a correct team takes control.
  5. In multiple choice, select four or six options, choose an answer, Lock in answer, then apply the score. Optional 50/50 leaves two choices and can be used once per team per game. The final round has no hints.
  6. Bonus Wagers require a wager before the question appears and are exclusive to the selecting team. The maximum is the larger of its score and the highest board value. A pass loses the wager.
  7. Final Challenge locks all wagers before revealing its question. Each team may wager 0 up to its positive score. Teams write responses privately; the host records and reviews them, then applies final scores. Wagers always gain or lose their full amount, even when normal penalties are off.
  8. Enable Host mode, then click Open player display. Move that window to a projector or second screen on this computer. It receives the board, questions, choices, scores, and timer. Answers stay private until Reveal last answer to players. Use host mode for private answer checking during steals.

Review last answer shows the answer and KJV reference after a clue ends. Undo restores the last score and previous turn; it can also undo final scoring. The bank contains hand-written trivia and generated scripture clues; difficulty ratings are estimates. New games reset scores and hints while retaining question history.
